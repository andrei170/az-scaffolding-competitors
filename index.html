Why this is the most annoying finding in the document

If they held cards and you did not, this would be a real competitive problem and an expensive one to fix. That is not the situation. You told us you hold them. The gap is not capability, it is that yours are in a folder and theirs are on a homepage.

A main contractor filling a package does not ring five scaffolders and ask. He opens five websites, and the ones that cannot evidence carded scaffolders and TG20 compliance in about four seconds do not make the shortlist. You are being filtered out before anyone speaks to you — and you would never know, because a call that never happens leaves no trace.

What Nobody In This Market Is Doing — Including Them

Not one firm in this comparison is measuring anything, and not one has an enquiry form on its homepage. Every single one of them relies on somebody finding a phone number and choosing to ring it.

That is the opportunity, and it is bigger than the accreditation gap. This is not a sophisticated market. Nobody is running measured advertising into a site built to convert, because nobody has a site built to convert. The first firm here that does it is not competing on a level field — it is playing a different game from the other three.

It also reframes what you said on the call about ten years without marketing. You have not fallen behind. Nobody has moved.

Where They Beat You On Basics
Two of the three have a tappable phone number. You do not.

EBN publishes a mobile as a tap-to-call link. Yorkshire Netting publishes a landline the same way. On a phone, one tap and it dials.

Your number is not a tel: link, and the audit already found there is no phone number, no form and no contact route on your homepage at all — a visitor has to click through to the contact page to find anything. That was the finding you said you did not know about.

Combine the two and the picture is uncomfortable: a builder comparing you against EBN on a phone can ring EBN in one tap and cannot ring you without effort. Most people do not make the effort. They ring the one that dialled.

The first two cards are the point. You did £682,000 last year with a website that cannot be contacted from its homepage and publishes none of your credentials. The two changes that close most of the gap on this page cost nothing but an afternoon — and they happen before any conversation about advertising. On the call you set a goal of roughly £100,000 a month. Nothing here gets you there on its own; all of it is the groundwork that has to exist first.

The Order We Would Do It In
  • Publish CISRS, NASC and CHAS on the homepage — every competitor does, you hold them, and it costs nothing
  • Put both numbers on every page as tap-to-call links — two of the three beat you on this today
  • Add your insurance figures and TG20 compliance — the two things a main contractor checks first
  • Ask ten recent customers for a Google review — ten years of goodwill, no public rating anywhere
Then — Where Nobody Else Is
  • Analytics and conversion tracking. None of the four has any. Being the only firm that can measure is a permanent advantage
  • An enquiry form that asks height, duration and site address — nobody in this comparison has a homepage form at all
  • Town pages for Pontefract, Castleford, Wakefield, Normanton, Knottingley and Featherstone — only Maine has made this move
  • A commercial page a main contractor can put through procurement
